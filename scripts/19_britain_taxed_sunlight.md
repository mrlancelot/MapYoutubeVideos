# Why Did Britain Tax Sunlight for 156 Years?
**Duration:** 40 seconds | **Style:** Reference Match

---

## Narration

Why did Britain tax sunlight for one hundred and fifty-six years? In 1696, King William III needed money for war. His solution was to tax windows. More windows meant more wealth, which meant more tax. The result was catastrophic for public health. People bricked up their windows to avoid paying. Entire rooms went dark. Buildings were designed with fewer windows from the start. The poor lived in permanent darkness because light cost money. Disease spread through dark, unventilated homes. The phrase daylight robbery was born from this tax. For over a century and a half, Britain literally taxed access to the sun. The window tax was not repealed until 1851. Walk through old British cities today and you can still see the bricked-up windows. That is eighteenth-century tax evasion preserved in stone.
