# How Did Europe Carve Up Africa Without Asking Africans?
**Duration:** 40 seconds | **Style:** Reference Match

---

## Narration

How did Europe carve up Africa without asking Africans? In 1884, representatives from fourteen European nations gathered in Berlin. They had come to divide an entire continent among themselves. No African was invited. Before this conference, Europeans controlled only ten percent of Africa, mostly coastal trading posts. But advances in medicine and weaponry had made the interior accessible. Britain, France, Germany, and Belgium all wanted their share. To avoid war with each other, they agreed on rules. Any European power could claim African territory simply by occupying it. What followed was a feeding frenzy. They drew borders with rulers, cutting through kingdoms and tribes without any regard for the people who lived there. By 1914, only Ethiopia remained independent. Those arbitrary borders still exist today, the cause of conflicts that continue more than a century later.
